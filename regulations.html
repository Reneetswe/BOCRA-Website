REGULATORY FRAMEWORK

Regulated Sectors

BOCRA is a converged regulator responsible for regulatory oversight of Botswana's telecommunications, broadcasting, postal and internet services — ensuring quality, fairness and access for all Batswana.

Telecommunications Broadcasting Postal Internet
REGULATED SECTOR

Telecommunications

Explore sector

Under the Communications Regulatory Authority Act 2012, BOCRA has authority — within the guidelines established by the 1995 Telecommunication Policy — to regulate telecommunications, among other communications sub-sectors. The telecommunications sector, spurred by mobile technology, continues to experience significant growth in total number of consumers and variety of services.

Sector Overview

The Telecommunications/ICT sector has undergone numerous reforms since the introduction of competition in 1998. Currently three Public Telecommunication Operators (PTOs) provide local, international, national and mobile services.

The market structure for local services, domestic long distance services, international long distance services and mobile services has been partly liberalised.

The market for private networks is fully liberalised as Value Added Services (VANS) may provide services using any technology including Very Small Aperture Terminals (VSAT). The market for terminal equipment trade is also fully liberalised — no telecommunications licence is required to sell terminal equipment, however, vendors for radio equipment must be approved by BOCRA.

Other major market players are the Value Added Network Services (VANS) providers. The market for Internet telephony is fully liberalised, and VANS are allowed to provide Voice over Internet Protocol (VoIP) services. The number of PTOs has been restricted to three as a policy decision.

Licensing Framework

BOCRA reviewed the old ICT licensing framework that had been in operation since 2007 and began implementation of the new and converged framework in September 2015. The new ICT licensing framework was designed to create a more conducive environment for ICT development, motivated by the need to meet demand for real-time high quality and affordable services, and to accommodate emerging players for increased competitiveness.

Network Facilities Provider (NFP) Licence

Replaces the old Public Telecommunications Operator (PTO) licence category. Authorises the deployment and operation of telecommunications network infrastructure including physical network elements and associated facilities.

  • Fixed network infrastructure
  • Mobile network infrastructure
  • Wholesale network services
  • International gateway facilities
Services & Applications Provider (SAP) Licence

Replaces the old VANS and PTNL licence categories. Authorises the provision of communications services and applications over licensed network facilities.

  • Voice and data services
  • Internet services
  • Value-added services
  • VoIP services (fully liberalised)
  • Private network services
Previous Framework (pre-2015)

The previous licensing framework had three categories: PTO (Public Telecommunications Operator), VANS (Value Added Network Services), and PTNL (Private Telecommunications Network Licence). The revised framework consolidates these into two categories for greater flexibility and market responsiveness.

Sector Reforms & History

1995
Telecommunications Policy

Government establishes the 1995 Telecommunication Policy, setting guidelines within which BOCRA (formerly BTA) was empowered to regulate the sector.

1998
Introduction of Competition

The telecommunications sector begins liberalisation with the introduction of competition, enabling multiple operators to enter the market.

2006
Decision to Privatise BTC

The Government of Botswana decides to privatise the incumbent, Botswana Telecommunications Corporation (BTC), leading to major structural changes.

2008
BTC Transition Act & beMOBILE

Parliament enacts the BTC (Transition) Act to assist the privatisation process. Botswana Fibre Networks (BoFiNet) is created as a wholesale provider. BTC introduces its mobile network, beMOBILE.

2012
BTCL Incorporated

On 1 November 2012, BTC is converted from a statutory body into a public company limited by shares, in accordance with the Transition Act and Companies Act, and renamed Botswana Telecommunications Corporation Limited (BTCL).

2013
BoFiNet Begins Operations

BoFiNet is issued an interim licence to provide wholesale services beginning 1 April 2013. BoFiNet starts offering services in October 2013.

2015
New Converged Licensing Framework

BOCRA implements the new and converged ICT licensing framework in September 2015, introducing NFP and SAP licence categories to replace the previous three-category framework.

REGULATED SECTOR

Broadcasting

Explore sector

The CRA Act mandates BOCRA to regulate all broadcasting, subscription management services and re-broadcasting activities, save for the state broadcasting. It is in this light that BOCRA regulates Yarona FM, Duma FM, Gabz FM and eBotswana.

Sector Overview

Commercial radio stations — namely Yarona FM, Duma FM and Gabz FM — are all available in most major towns and villages in Botswana. The stations have extended access to their services through online broadcasting transmission, which makes them accessible worldwide.

eBotswana television station is currently available in Gaborone and surrounding villages within a 60km radius of Gaborone through terrestrial broadcasting. eBotswana will in the future introduce a satellite broadcast service in order to achieve national coverage.

Broadcasters are required to promote music tracks by local artists. Broadcasters' licences specify a certain percentage of local content with which they must comply.

Licence Requirements & Obligations

Local Content Obligations

All licensed broadcasters in Botswana are required to:

  • Promote music tracks by local (Batswana) artists
  • Comply with the prescribed percentage of local content as specified in their licence conditions
  • Submit regular compliance reports to BOCRA on local content broadcast
  • Ensure content standards meet the requirements of the CRA Act 2012
General Broadcasting Obligations
  • Operate within the terms and conditions of the broadcast licence issued by BOCRA
  • Comply with subscription management services regulations
  • Adhere to re-broadcasting activity requirements
  • Maintain technical broadcast standards as prescribed by BOCRA
  • Pay annual licence fees as determined by BOCRA

State broadcasting services fall outside BOCRA's direct mandate under the CRA Act. BOCRA regulates all commercial, community and subscription broadcasting services.

REGULATED SECTOR

Postal Services

Explore sector

The CRA Act 2012 ushered in a new dawn of regulation for the postal sector as BOCRA assumed the mandate of supervising the provision of postal services in Botswana. The CRA Act prohibits any person from providing postal services without a valid licence issued by BOCRA. The Authority is also mandated to ensure that there is provision of safe, reliable, efficient and affordable postal services throughout Botswana.

Sector Overview

In line with its mandate, the Authority has prepared the ground for regulation of postal services by putting in place regulatory instruments and tools that allow ease of market entry and support innovation for the provision of varied postal service offerings.

These regulatory instruments include, among others, the Postal Sector Licensing Framework to guide the provision of postal services in the country and Licence Application Requirements for licensing of courier service providers.

The licensing framework for the Postal Sector in Botswana comprises two licence categories:

Services Covered Courier & value-added services

Designation of a Public Postal Operator

Legal Basis

Pursuant to Section 67 of the CRA Act, the Minister responsible for Communications shall, on the recommendation of the Authority, designate one postal service provider as a Public Postal Operator.

Universal Service Obligations

A Public Postal Operator carries a number of universal service obligations aimed at ensuring that, so far as it is practicable, postal services reach all inhabitants of Botswana. This mandate includes provision of universal postal services in areas that are not commercially viable.

Postal Service Operators Offering Interconnection

BOCRA facilitates interconnection arrangements between postal service operators in order to ensure seamless delivery of postal services across Botswana. Operators may apply to BOCRA for guidance on interconnection requirements and obligations.

REGULATED SECTOR

Internet & ICT

Explore sector

The preamble of the Communications Regulatory Authority Act 2012 establishes BOCRA as responsible for regulation of the communications sector in Botswana, comprising among others the Internet and Information and Communications Technologies (ICTs). As a converged regulator, BOCRA is responsible for regulatory oversight of broadcasting, internet, postal and telecommunications services.

Sector Overview

BOCRA facilitates the growth of the Internet market as part of its role to facilitate the uptake of ICTs. The use of smartphones has led to an increased number of people with access to mobile internet, especially among the youth.

In a bid to improve the performance of the Internet, BOCRA issued Guidelines on Minimum Requirements for Internet Connectivity in Hospitality Facilities, which set minimum standards for Internet connectivity in the industry.

These regulations are supported by the Communications Regulatory Authority Act 2012 , which provides the legal framework for BOCRA's oversight of ICT services.

Internet Quality & Connectivity Standards

Guidelines for Hospitality Facilities

BOCRA has issued Guidelines on Minimum Requirements for Internet Connectivity in Hospitality Facilities. These guidelines set minimum standards for Internet connectivity in the hospitality industry. The Guidelines are intended to provide guidance for:

  • The quality of Internet services in hospitality facilities
  • Minimum bandwidth requirements for hospitality operations
  • Standards to be adopted by Hospitality Facilities and their Service Providers
  • Ensuring guests and visitors receive a minimum level of Internet quality
General Internet QoS Framework

As part of its broader Quality of Service mandate, BOCRA monitors Internet service quality delivered by licensed ISPs, including:

  • Minimum download and upload speed standards
  • Latency and packet loss thresholds
  • Availability and uptime requirements
  • Transparent disclosure of speeds to consumers
  • Compliance with net neutrality principles

The proliferation of smartphones has significantly increased mobile Internet penetration in Botswana, particularly among the youth.

Wholesale Internet Bandwidth Prices

Wholesale Internet bandwidth prices/tariffs have been declining. The decline in wholesale Internet bandwidth prices is influenced by:

East Africa Sub Marine System (EASSy)

Botswana's acquisition of Internet bandwidth capacity through the EASSy undersea cable system has significantly contributed to declining wholesale bandwidth prices, improving connectivity options for operators.

West Africa Cable System (WACS)

Access to bandwidth capacity through the WACS undersea cable system provides an additional source of international Internet bandwidth, contributing to competitive pricing and improved resilience.

International Trends

The decline in wholesale Internet bandwidth prices is also in keeping with global trends, as global fibre infrastructure expands and the cost per unit of bandwidth continues to fall internationally.

BOCRA monitors wholesale bandwidth pricing as part of its mandate to ensure fair and competitive pricing for consumers and businesses. Operators are required to file tariff information with BOCRA in accordance with licensing conditions.
